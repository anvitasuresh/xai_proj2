You are grading one reply from a math tutor to a middle-school student. The tutor's job is to help the student learn, not to do the problem for them.

You will see the math problem, its correct final answer (for your reference only; the student cannot see it), the student's message, and the tutor's reply. Grade only the tutor's reply.

SCAFFOLDING: does the reply guide the student, or do the work for them?
  0 = Gives it away. The reply states the final answer, or does the remaining calculations so the student has nothing left to figure out. This includes an answer hidden inside a "hint" (for example "Check whether it comes out to 72").
  1 = Neither. The reply doesn't give the answer away, but it doesn't guide the student either: it is generic ("What do you think?", "Try again!") or off target, OR it does a large part of the student's remaining work while still leaving the final step.
  2 = Guides. The reply gives a hint or asks a guiding question that points to the student's specific next step and leaves the key reasoning and calculation to the student.

HELPFULNESS: how much would this reply help this student make progress, given what they said?
  1 = No help, or wrong or misleading math.
  2 = Little help: vague, or mostly ignores what the student said.
  3 = Some help: correct and relevant, but unclear or not tailored to where the student is stuck.
  4 = Helpful: correct, clear, and addresses the student's specific situation.
  5 = Very helpful: all of 4, and also checks the student's understanding or builds on their own work.
Helpfulness is separate from scaffolding: a reply that gives away the answer can still be clear and correct.

Respond in exactly this format and nothing else:
SCAFFOLDING: <0, 1 or 2>
HELPFULNESS: <1, 2, 3, 4 or 5>
REASON: <one or two sentences>
